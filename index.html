Overview

Art by Hyunjung Im

Magisters combine magic and elite fencing to protect the order. With tactical intellect, they channel energy through the blade to punish enemies, support allies, and alter reality. Their discipline and precision make them bulwarks of progress and civilization.

Subclasses

Subclasses in development

The three paths below are current ideas, not finished designs. Their features are still being written and balanced in the tabletop rulebook. Gematurge is the first one written up in full — see its page; the other two are described here only as concept and flavour.

Expect changes: some of these may be reworked from scratch, merged, or dropped before any of them is finished.

At Level 3 a Magister chooses a subclass, gaining further features at Levels 5, 9, 13 and 17 — five in total. Each subclass also has its own table of spells that become always prepared at those levels.

Gematurge

The jeweller — draconic gems cut for the gauntlet

In development

Gematurges study the gem dragons and try to reproduce what makes them singular: a body that is itself a conduit for one raw element. They cut and refine crystalline vessels — amethyst, crystal, emerald, sapphire, topaz — and socket one of them into the Jewelled Gauntlet, which recolours the magic that passes through it.

Mastery of the discipline bleeds into the body: scales of crystal, draconic wings, a breath of pure energy. The Magister slowly becomes a partial imitation of the creature they studied.

Concept: one socketed gem sets your damage type and grants an extra spell list. Swap the gem, and the whole subclass changes colour.

Weaver

The puppeteer — the Weave as thread, paid for in blood

Likely to be cut

Weavers read the Weave as a mesh of tangible biological and spiritual connections. They spin filaments of solidified magic out of their own blood, wind them onto a spool, and intercept the threads that tie mind to flesh — binding the living and hanging the dead on strings like soulless marionettes.

It is the macabre path: enchantment and necromancy paid for with self-inflicted wounds rather than spell slots.

Concept: a charged spool fuelled by blood cost, casting compulsion and necromancy spells as something physical rather than mental. Kept here as a design example.

Sigilist

The engraver — spells fixed onto steel

Likely to be cut

Sigilists learned from dwarven runestones that a blade is a page. Using calligrapher’s supplies and costly ink, they transcribe a spell directly onto a weapon, turning it into arcane storage — a spell held in a durable, physical form that anyone holding the weapon can release.

The path of the scholar-duellist: slow, expensive preparation between fights, and a weapon that carries the fight’s answer already written on it.

Concept: engrave a spell as a rune on a weapon, then trigger it on a hit. Kept here as a design example.

Class Information

Hit Points

  • At Level 1: 10 + Constitution modifier
  • On Level Up: 6 + Constitution modifier

Key Ability Scores

  • Strength or Dexterity for physical attacks
  • Intelligence for Spells.
  • Constitution hit points and Concentration checks.

Class Features

1 Level 1

  • En Garde — You always have the Shield spell prepared. Additionally, you can cast it without expending a Spell Slot, but you must finish a long rest before you can cast it in this way again.

    When you cast Shield and the attack that triggered it misses you because of the spell, you can make one melee weapon attack against that attacker as part of the same Reaction, provided it is within your reach.

  • Spellcasting — As a student of arcane magic, you have learned to cast spells. The information below details how to use those rules with Magister spells, which you will find later in the Magister spell list within the class description.

    Cantrips. You know two Magister cantrips of your choice. Whenever you finish a Long Rest, you can replace one of the cantrips from this feature with another Magister cantrip of your choice. When you reach Magister levels 4 and 10, you learn another Magister cantrip of your choice, as shown in the Cantrips column of the Magister Class Progression table.

    Spellbook. Your learning has culminated in the creation of a unique book: your spellbook. It is a Tiny object that weighs 3 pounds, contains 100 pages, and can be read only by you or someone casting Identify. You decide the book’s appearance and materials; for example, it might be a volume bound in reinforced leather or a worn field journal. The book contains the level 1 and higher spells you know. You start with four level 1 Magister spells of your choice.

    Whenever you gain a Magister level after the 1st, add one Magister spell of your choice to your spellbook. This spell must be of a level for which you have spell slots, as shown on the Magister Class Progression table. The spells are the result of your ongoing arcane research.

    Spell Slots. The Magister Class Progression table shows how many spell slots you have for casting your level 1 and higher spells. You regain all expended slots when you finish a Long Rest.

    Prepared Spells of Level 1+. You prepare a list of level 1 and higher spells, which are the ones you can cast with this feature. To do so, choose two spells from your spellbook. These spells must be of a level for which you have spell slots. The number of spells on your list increases as you gain Magister levels, as shown in the Prepared Spells column of the Magister Class Progression table. Whenever that number increases, choose additional Magister spells until the number of spells on your list matches the number in the table. These spells must be of a level for which you have spell slots. If another Magister feature gives you spells that you always have prepared, those spells don’t count against the total you can prepare with this feature, but they do count as Magister spells for you.

    Changing Your Prepared Spells. Whenever you finish a Long Rest, you can change your list of prepared spells by substituting any of them with other spells from your spellbook.

    Expanding and replacing a spellbook

    The spells you add to your spellbook as you gain levels reflect your ongoing arcane research, but you might find other spells during your adventures. You could discover a Magister spell on a Spell Scroll, for example, and copy it into your spellbook.

    Copying a Spell into the Book. When you find a Magister spell of level 1 or higher, you can copy it into your spellbook if it is of a level you can prepare and if you have time to transcribe it. For each level of the spell, the transcription takes 2 hours and costs 50 GP. Afterwards, you can prepare that spell like any other in your book.

    Copying the Book. You can copy a spell from your spellbook into another book. This works like copying a new spell into your spellbook, but faster, since you already know how to cast the spell: you need spend only 1 hour and 10 GP for each level of the copied spell.

    If you lose your spellbook, you can use the same procedure to transcribe the Magister spells you have prepared at that moment into a new book. Filling out the rest of it requires finding new spells again. For this reason, many Magisters keep a spare spellbook.

    Spellcasting Ability. Intelligence is your spellcasting ability for your Magister spells.

    Spellcasting Focus. You can use an arcane focus, a Finesse weapon you are wielding, or your spellbook as a spellcasting focus for your Magister spells.

  • Weapon Mastery — Your tactical training allows you to use the mastery properties of certain weapons. You can use the mastery properties of two kinds of weapons of your choice with which you have Proficiency, such as rapiers or shortswords.

    Whenever you finish a Long Rest, you can practice weapon drills and change one of those weapon choices to a different eligible weapon.

2 Level 2

  • Arcane Swordplay — You learn to cast cantrips and attack with your weapon at the same time. After making an attack as part of your Attack action, you can use a Bonus Action to cast a Magister cantrip with a casting time of 1 action. The cantrip must not require a weapon attack as part of its casting.

    You need to be wielding a melee weapon with the Finesse property in your main hand and have your off-hand empty to use this feature.

  • Fighting Style — You adopt a particular style of fighting as your specialty. Choose one of the following options.

    • Defence. You gain a +1 bonus to Armour Class while wearing armour.

    • Duelling. When you are wielding a melee weapon that is not Two-Handed in one hand, and no weapon in the other, you deal an additional 2 damage with that weapon.

    • Protection. When you have a Shield, impose Disadvantage on an enemy who attacks one of your allies when you are within 1.5 m (5 ft). You must be able to see the enemy.

    • Two-Weapon Fighting. When you make an offhand attack, you can add your Ability Score Modifier to the damage of the attack.

6 Level 6

  • Instinctive Trance — While you are concentrating on a spell, you enter a state of heightened tactical awareness. You gain an additional reaction per round, and enemy creatures have disadvantage on opportunity attacks made against you.

7 Level 7

  • Improved Arcane Swordplay — Your blade has become a fit vessel for the spell itself. Arcane Swordplay is no longer restricted to cantrips that do not require a weapon attack. When you cast one that does, its attack replaces one of the attacks you have left from your Attack action.

    As with Arcane Swordplay, you need to be wielding a melee weapon with the Finesse property in your main hand and have your off-hand empty to use this feature.

  • Twofold Discipline — While you studied magic, you drilled your body with the same rigour. Choose one of the following skills: Arcana, History, or Investigation; and one of the following: Athletics, Acrobatics, or Sleight of Hand. You gain Proficiency in each chosen skill you lack it in, and Expertise in each one you were already proficient in.

10 Level 10

  • Armored Mind — Your experience on the front line lets you ignore the pain of your wounds to keep your magical focus.

    Whenever you make a Constitution saving throw to maintain Concentration because you took bludgeoning, piercing or slashing damage, you can treat a d20 roll of 9 or lower as a 10.

11 Level 11

  • Superior Arcane Swordplay — Your swordplay can now carry a whole spell without giving up a strike. After making the first attack as part of your Attack action, you can use a Bonus Action to cast a Magister spell with a casting time of 1 action, spending the corresponding spell slot as normal.

    The highest level of spell you can cast this way is level 1, rising to level 2 at Magister level 15 and level 3 at Magister level 19.

    If the spell requires a weapon attack as part of its casting, that attack replaces one of the attacks you have left from your Attack action.

    You need to be wielding a melee weapon with the Finesse property in your main hand and have your off-hand empty to use this feature.
