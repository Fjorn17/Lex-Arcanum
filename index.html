Overview

Art by Hyunjung Im

Magisters combine magic and elite fencing to protect the order. With tactical intellect, they channel energy through the blade to punish enemies, support allies, and alter reality. Their discipline and precision make them bulwarks of progress and civilization.

Subclasses

Subclasses in development

The three paths below are current ideas, not finished designs. Their features are still being written and balanced in the tabletop rulebook, and the summaries here describe only the concept and the flavour — no rules text, no numbers.

Expect changes: some of these may be reworked from scratch, merged, or dropped before any of them is implemented for Baldur’s Gate 3.

At Level 3 a Magister chooses a subclass, gaining further features at Levels 5 and 9 — three in total. Each one takes the Arcane Gauntlet in a different direction: what the Magister makes with it, and what the law of the Lex Arcanum lets them do with what they made.

Gematurge

The jeweller — draconic gems cut for the gauntlet

In development

Gematurges study the gem dragons and try to reproduce what makes them singular: a body that is itself a conduit for one raw element. They cut and refine crystalline vessels — amethyst, crystal, emerald, sapphire, topaz — and socket one of them into the Arcane Gauntlet, which recolours the magic that passes through it.

Mastery of the discipline bleeds into the body: scales of crystal, draconic wings, a breath of pure energy. The Magister slowly becomes a partial imitation of the creature they studied.

Concept: one socketed gem sets your damage type and grants an extra spell list. Swap the gem, and the whole subclass changes colour.

Weaver

The puppeteer — the Weave as thread, paid for in blood

Likely to be cut

Weavers read the Weave as a mesh of tangible biological and spiritual connections. They spin filaments of solidified magic out of their own blood, wind them onto a spool, and intercept the threads that tie mind to flesh — binding the living and hanging the dead on strings like soulless marionettes.

It is the macabre path: enchantment and necromancy paid for with self-inflicted wounds rather than spell slots.

Concept: a charged spool fuelled by blood cost, casting compulsion and necromancy spells as something physical rather than mental. Kept here as a design example.

Sigilist

The engraver — spells fixed onto steel

Likely to be cut

Sigilists learned from dwarven runestones that a blade is a page. Using calligrapher’s supplies and costly ink, they transcribe a spell directly onto a weapon, turning it into arcane storage — a spell held in a durable, physical form that anyone holding the weapon can release.

The path of the scholar-duellist: slow, expensive preparation between fights, and a weapon that carries the fight’s answer already written on it.

Concept: engrave a spell as a rune on a weapon, then trigger it on a hit. Kept here as a design example.

The Arcane Gauntlet

A Magister casts through an Arcane Gauntlet and nothing else — no staff, no orb, no other arcane focus. The gauntlet hand must be completely free to shape somatic components.

This one requirement is the spine of the class. One hand holds the gauntlet, the other a Finesse blade, which means no shield, no two-handed weapon, no dual wielding, no hand crossbow while casting. Every restriction the Magister lives under follows from it, and so does the silhouette: a duellist with an open casting hand held back like a fencer’s.

Schemas

A Schema is a perfected combat pattern that interweaves magical theory with physical mastery — a specialised method that lets you execute a tactical manoeuvre when you cast a particular spell or cantrip.

Requirements. Every Schema requires a specific Magister spell or cantrip to be prepared. If you stop having that spell prepared, the Schema does nothing until you prepare it again. Some Schemas carry further requirements, such as a minimum Magister level or another Schema.

Learning and swapping. You know 2 Schemas at Level 1 and gain more at Levels 5 and 9, to a maximum of 4. Each time you gain a Magister level you may replace one known Schema with another you qualify for — but not one that another of your Schemas depends on. You cannot take the same Schema twice unless it says otherwise.

Level progression

1 Level 1

  • Spellcasting — Intelligence is your spellcasting ability. You know 2 cantrips and prepare spells from the Magister list — you are a prepared caster, not a known caster. You may swap one cantrip and one prepared spell each time you gain a Magister level. Your focus must be an Arcane Gauntlet; no staff, orb or other arcane focus works, and the gauntlet hand must be completely free to perform somatic components.
  • Schemas — Learn 2 Schemas — combat patterns that let you execute a tactical manoeuvre when you cast a particular spell or cantrip. Every Schema requires a specific Magister spell to be prepared; while it is not prepared, the Schema does nothing. You may replace one Schema each time you gain a Magister level, except one that another Schema of yours depends on.

2 Level 2

  • Fighting Style — Take a Fighting Style feat, or Arcane Warrior: learn two Wizard cantrips that count as Magister spells and use Intelligence. You may swap one of them each Magister level.
  • Cantrip Combat — Requires the Arcane Gauntlet in one hand and a melee weapon with the Finesse property in the other.
    When you take the Attack action, you may choose to add no ability modifier to the damage of every attack in that action (unless the modifier is negative). If you do, you may spend your Bonus Action to cast a Magister cantrip with a casting time of 1 action. If the cantrip itself calls for a weapon attack, that attack adds no modifier either. You may cast it immediately after the Attack action or between its attacks.

3 Level 3

  • Magister Subclass — Choose a subclass. It grants features now and again at Levels 5 and 9.

4 Level 4

  • Feat — Take a Feat.

5 Level 5

  • Extra Attack — You can make an additional weapon attack after making an unarmed or weapon attack.

6 Level 6

  • Spell Combat — Requires the Arcane Gauntlet in one hand and a melee weapon with the Finesse property in the other.
    When you take the Attack action, you may choose to add no ability modifier to the damage of every attack in that action (unless the modifier is negative). If you do, you may spend your Bonus Action to cast a Magister spell with a casting time of 1 action, spending the spell slot as normal. If the spell calls for a weapon attack, that attack adds no modifier either. You may cast it immediately after the Attack action or between its attacks.
    The highest spell level you can cast this way rises with your Magister level:
    1st at Magister 6 · 2nd at 10

7 Level 7

  • Intellectual Authority — Add your Intelligence modifier to Charisma (Persuasion) and Charisma (Deception) checks.

8 Level 8

  • Feat — Take a Feat.

9 Level 9

  • Subclass feature — Learn 1 additional Schema (4 total). You gain access to Level 3 spells.

10 Level 10

  • Steel Mind — Whenever you make a Constitution saving throw to maintain Concentration because you took bludgeoning, piercing or slashing damage, you may treat a d20 result of 9 or lower as a 10.

11 Level 11

  • Magic Swordsman — When you use Cantrip Combat or Spell Combat, you may add your ability modifier to the damage of the first weapon attack that hits that turn, ignoring those features’ penalty for that attack.

12 Level 12

  • Feat — Take a Feat.
