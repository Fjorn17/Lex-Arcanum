Marionettes

Item category · figures worked on a cross

In development

An item category, not a class feature. Statistics, prices and tier names are drafts.

What a marionette is

A marionette is a jointed figure hung from a wooden cross — the control bar the strings run to. Anyone can own one. Anyone with the right hands can make one move.

Marionettes come in two families. Sewn figures are the ordinary kind: carved wood, pinned hinges and stitched cloth, light enough to carry rolled in a case. Plated figures are the other kind — a suit of armour built to be worked from the outside, standing as tall as the person holding the cord and hitting like it.

They are equipment, and they behave like it: bought, commissioned, inherited, stolen, smashed in bar fights and repaired badly. Nothing about owning one requires a class, a subclass, or the order's permission.

Who can work one

Sewn figures need proficiency with weaver's tools and carpenter's tools — both. The figure is carpentry and the strings are weaving, and a cord you cannot read is a cord you cannot pull.

Plated figures need proficiency with marionettes, and each such item says so on its own entry, the way a suit of magic plate says it needs heavy armour training. Without that proficiency the figure hangs dead on the line: you can lift it, and that is all.

The Threadmarshal grants both, which is why the discipline and the craft are usually found together — but a fighter who took the proficiency another way works a plated figure just as well.

Working a marionette

While you are holding a marionette's cross and have the proficiency its entry requires, you can command it as a Bonus Action: it moves up to its Speed and takes one action from among those its tier allows.

It does nothing on a turn in which you do not command it, and it must stay within 18 m (60 ft) of you or it goes slack and drops where it stands. You can work only one marionette at a time, however many you own.

A marionette uses your Proficiency Bonus. When it makes an attack roll, it uses your spell attack modifier; when a creature makes a saving throw against something it does, the DC is your spell save DC.

Tiers

Marionettes follow the ordinary rules of the Compendium of Arcane Artisanship for their rarity, crafted with weaver's tools and carpenter's tools together.

Sewn figures cannot take the Attack action at any rarity. They are there to get in the way, hold a rope, reach a latch and give someone something to worry about. Proficiency required: weaver's tools and carpenter's tools.

Plated figures can take the Attack action, and every one of them carries the line “Requires proficiency with marionettes” on its entry. They also take the actions any sewn figure of the same rarity or lower could take. A plated figure is Medium, weighs what the armour weighs, and cannot be carried rolled up in a case.

Traits

Strength. Sewn figures use Strength 8; plated figures use Strength 18.

Carved Body. The marionette needs no air, food, drink or sleep, and is unaffected by anything that targets only living creatures. It is an object when nobody is working it.

Slack. It acts only when the creature holding its cross commands it, and drops where it stands if that creature is more than 18 m (60 ft) away, releases the cross, or has the Incapacitated condition.

Climbs Its Own String (sewn, Uncommon and up). It can climb its own strings, and any surface they are anchored to, including upside down along a ceiling, without an ability check.

Interpose (plated). When a creature its worker can see attacks a target within 1.5 m (5 ft) of the figure, the worker can use a Reaction to have the figure step into the blow: the attack targets the figure instead.

Actions

The marionette takes only the action its worker commands, chosen from those its tier allows. A sewn figure can never take the Attack action.

Damage and repair

A marionette reduced to 0 Hit Points comes apart. The pieces are still worth something: rebuilding a figure from its own wreckage costs a quarter of its creation cost and a quarter of its time, and can be done by anyone with the two tool proficiencies.

Mending repairs a marionette that is merely broken, but does nothing for one reduced to 0 Hit Points: at that point the problem is not a crack, it is that the figure is in pieces across the floor.

Marionettes are ordinary equipment. The Threadmarshal grants the proficiencies needed to work every kind, but the category stands on its own.
