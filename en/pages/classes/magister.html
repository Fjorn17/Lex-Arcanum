Magister

A half-caster of the Lex Arcanum — Intelligence, finesse blades, and spells woven into the swordplay.

“Every duel is an argument. I merely write mine down before I win it.”

Overview

Art by Hyunjung Im

Magisters are bound by the Lex Arcanum, the written body of law by which their order holds magic to account. Tribunal duelists, circuit magistrates, order-appointed champions, investigators sent where a writ alone will not reach — all of them are Magisters, and all of them answer to the same code. What sets a Magister apart from any other arcane scholar is not the strength of their magic but the discipline of it: nothing they cast is improvised, and nothing they do goes unrecorded.

A Magister trains with the blade as rigorously as with the book, and fights with both at once — a finesse weapon in one hand, a spell in the other. The order’s fencing is codified down to the last measure, taught from manuals and drilled until it answers faster than thought, and its magic is set down the same way: every Magister keeps a spellbook of their own research, added to after every case and every duel. The steel opens the argument. The free hand settles it.

No Magister stays a generalist for long. Each takes up one of the order’s disciplines — cutting draconic gems, spinning the Weave into thread, engraving spells onto steel — and it is that specialization, laid over a common training in law, arcana and the sword, that decides what kind of duelist they become. Few of them remain behind tribunal walls. The order’s writ runs only as far as its Magisters carry it, which is why so many of them are found on the road.

Becoming a Magister…

As a Level 1 Character

  • You gain all the traits in the Core Magister Traits table.
  • You gain the Magister’s level 1 features, which are listed in the Magister Features Table.

As a Multiclass Character

  • You gain the following traits from the Core Magister Traits table: Hit Point Die, proficiency with   Simple weapons, plus Scimitar,  Shortsword,  Longsword,  and  Rapier,   and training with Light  and Medium  armor.
  • You gain the Magister’s level 1 features, which are listed in the Magister Features Table.

Magister Class Features

1 Level 1

  • En Garde — The order's first lesson is that a parry is only half an answer.

    Ready Guard. You always have the shield spell prepared.

    Free Parry. You can cast shield without expending a spell slot. Once you do so, you can't do so again until you finish a Long Rest.

    Riposte. When an attacker misses you with an attack because of your shield spell, you can make one melee weapon attack against that attacker as part of the same Reaction. The attacker must be within your reach.

  • Spellcasting — As a student of arcane magic, you have learned to cast spells. The information below details how to use those rules with Magister spells, which you will find later in the Magister Spell List within the class description.

    Cantrips. You know two Magister cantrips of your choice. Whenever you finish a Long Rest, you can replace one of the cantrips from this feature with another Magister cantrip of your choice. When you reach Magister levels 4 and 10, you learn another Magister cantrip of your choice, as shown in the Cantrips column of the Magister Features Table.

    Spellbook. Your learning has culminated in the creation of a unique book: your spellbook. It is a Tiny object that weighs 3 pounds, contains 100 pages, and can be read only by you or someone casting Identify. You decide the book’s appearance and materials; for example, it might be a volume bound in reinforced leather or a worn field journal. The book contains the level 1 and higher spells you know. You start with four level 1 Magister spells of your choice.

    Whenever you gain a Magister level after the 1st, add one Magister spell of your choice to your spellbook. This spell must be of a level for which you have spell slots, as shown on the Magister Features Table. The spells are the result of your ongoing arcane research.

    Spell Slots. The Magister Features Table shows how many spell slots you have for casting your level 1 and higher spells. You regain all expended slots when you finish a Long Rest.

    Prepared Spells of Level 1+. You prepare a list of level 1 and higher spells, which are the ones you can cast with this feature. To do so, choose two spells from your spellbook. These spells must be of a level for which you have spell slots. The number of spells on your list increases as you gain Magister levels, as shown in the Prepared Spells column of the Magister Features Table. Whenever that number increases, choose additional Magister spells until the number of spells on your list matches the number in the table. These spells must be of a level for which you have spell slots. If another Magister feature gives you spells that you always have prepared, those spells don’t count against the total you can prepare with this feature, but they do count as Magister spells for you.

    Changing Your Prepared Spells. Whenever you finish a Long Rest, you can change your list of prepared spells by substituting any of them with other spells from your spellbook.

    Expanding and replacing a spellbook

    The spells you add to your spellbook as you gain levels reflect your ongoing arcane research, but you might find other spells during your adventures. You could discover a Magister spell on a Spell Scroll, for example, and copy it into your spellbook.

    Copying a Spell into the Book. When you find a Magister spell of level 1 or higher, you can copy it into your spellbook if it is of a level you can prepare and if you have time to transcribe it. For each level of the spell, the transcription takes 2 hours and costs 50 GP. Afterwards, you can prepare that spell like any other in your book.

    Copying the Book. You can copy a spell from your spellbook into another book. This works like copying a new spell into your spellbook, but faster, since you already know how to cast the spell: you need spend only 1 hour and 10 GP for each level of the copied spell.

    If you lose your spellbook, you can use the same procedure to transcribe the Magister spells you have prepared at that moment into a new book. Filling out the rest of it requires finding new spells again. For this reason, many Magisters keep a spare spellbook.

    Spellcasting Ability. Intelligence is your spellcasting ability for your Magister spells.

    Spellcasting Focus. You can use an arcane focus or your spellbook as a spellcasting focus for your Magister spells.

  • Weapon Mastery — Your tactical training allows you to use the mastery properties of certain weapons. You can use the mastery properties of two kinds of weapons of your choice with which you have Proficiency, such as rapiers or shortswords.

    Whenever you finish a Long Rest, you can practice weapon drills and change one of those weapon choices to a different eligible weapon.

2 Level 2

  • Arcane Swordplay — You fight with a weapon in one hand and a spell in the other.

    Woven Cantrip. When you take the Attack action on your turn and attack with a weapon that has the Finesse property, you can cast one Magister cantrip as a Bonus Action later on the same turn.

    The cantrip must have a casting time of an action, and it must not require a weapon attack as part of its casting.

    Woven Spell. Starting at Magister level 7, you can cast a Magister spell of level 1 or lower this way instead of a cantrip, expending a spell slot as normal. That maximum level increases to 2 at Magister level 14 and to 3 at Magister level 20.

  • Fighting Style —You gain a Fighting Style feat of your choice. Instead of choosing one of those feats, you can choose the option below.

    • Ritual Warrior — You learn one level 1 Magister spell of your choice that has the Ritual tag (see Magister Spell List). Bound Weapon is recommended. The chosen spell is added to your spellbook. You can cast any spell in your spellbook as a Ritual if that spell has the Ritual tag, even if you don't have it prepared, but you must read from the book to cast a spell in this way.

3 Level 3

  • Magister Subclass — You gain a Magister subclass of your choice: a specialization that grants you features again at Magister levels 7, 15 and 20. See Subclasses.

  • Twofold Discipline — Choose one skill from Arcana, History, or Investigation, and one skill from Acrobatics, Athletics, or Sleight of Hand. You gain proficiency in a chosen skill if you lack it, or Expertise in it if you already have proficiency.

4 Level 4

  • Ability Score Improvement — You gain the Ability Score Improvement feat or another feat of your choice for which you qualify.

5 Level 5

  • Extra Attack — When you take the Attack action on your turn, you can make two attacks instead of one.

6 Level 6

  • Unquestionable Authority — Your time among bureaucrats and tribunals has sharpened your mind like a blade.

    When you make a Persuasion or Deception check, you add your Intelligence modifier to the check.

7 Level 7

  • Subclass Feature — You gain a feature from your chosen Magister subclass. See Subclasses.

  • Instinctive Trance — While you are concentrating on a spell and you are not wearing heavy armor, you enter a state of heightened tactical awareness.

    On each of your turns while the trance lasts, you can take one additional Reaction.

    You can use this feature a number of times equal to your Intelligence modifier (minimum of once), and you regain all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest.

8 Level 8

  • Ability Score Improvement — You gain the Ability Score Improvement feat or another feat of your choice for which you qualify.

9 Level 9

  • Touché — The riposte stops being a reminder and starts being the point.

    When you hit with the melee weapon attack granted by En Garde, the attack deals extra 1d8 Force damage per level of the spell slot you expended to cast shield, plus your Intelligence modifier.

    If you cast shield without expending a spell slot, treat it as a level 1 slot for this feature.

10 Level 10

  • Armored Mind — Your experience on the front line lets you ignore the pain of your wounds to keep your magical focus.

    When you make a Constitution saving throw to maintain Concentration because you took Bludgeoning, Piercing, or Slashing damage, you can treat a d20 roll of 9 or lower as a 10.

11 Level 11

  • Spellsword — The spell stops arriving after the blade and starts arriving as it.

    Blade Casting. When you take the Attack action on your turn, you can replace one of the attacks with casting a Magister cantrip or spell that requires a weapon attack as part of its casting. Casting a spell this way expends a spell slot as normal.

    The spell must have a casting time of an action, and it must be of a level you can cast with Arcane Swordplay.

    One or the Other. You can use this feature only once on each of your turns, and not on a turn in which you use Arcane Swordplay.

12 Level 12

  • Ability Score Improvement — You gain the Ability Score Improvement feat or another feat of your choice for which you qualify.

14 Level 14

  • Esoteric Archives — You have learned to decipher arcane formulas from the wizard tradition.

    Copying Wizard Spells. When you find a spell of level 3 or lower from the Wizard spell list on a Spell Scroll, you can copy it into your spellbook. The time and gold required to copy it are halved.

    Esoteric Preparation. Whenever you finish a Long Rest, you can prepare a number of those spells from your spellbook equal to your Intelligence modifier (minimum of one spell). They count as Magister spells for you and don't count against the number of spells you can prepare.

15 Level 15

  • Subclass Feature — You gain a feature from your chosen Magister subclass. See Subclasses.

16 Level 16

  • Ability Score Improvement — You gain the Ability Score Improvement feat or another feat of your choice for which you qualify.

18 Level 18

  • Perfect Concentration — While your Instinctive Trance is active, you can use your Reaction to take the Dodge action when you are about to make an attack roll, when you are about to be the target of an attack roll, or when you are about to make a Dexterity saving throw.

    In addition, you now regain all expended uses of your Instinctive Trance when you finish a Short or Long Rest.

19 Level 19

  • Epic Boon — You gain an Epic Boon feat of your choice for which you qualify.

20 Level 20

  • Subclass Feature — You gain a feature from your chosen Magister subclass. See Subclasses.
